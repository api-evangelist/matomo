<p>Privacy regulations across Europe are evolving. 2026 is shaping up to be a pivotal year in the privacy world.</p>



<p>For analytics teams, compliance leaders, and digital decision-makers, the next 12–18 months will bring concrete changes to how audience measurement can be configured, justified, and audited.</p>



<p>If your organisation relies on web analytics without consent, or is actively trying to reduce consent friction while staying compliant, these updates matter.</p>



<p>From France&#8217;s new CNIL self-assessment framework to the EU&#8217;s Digital Omnibus initiative and the UK&#8217;s updated PECR rules, significant changes are on the horizon. Some of these could make privacy-first analytics easier to use without consent. Others raise important questions about the future of data protection.</p>



<p>Here&#8217;s what you need to know, what is changing, and how Matomo is preparing ahead of time.&nbsp;</p>



<div class="wp-block-spacer" style="height: 40px;"></div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">France: CNIL moves to a self-assessment framework for consent exemption</h2>



<p>The <a href="https://matomo.org/blog/2025/09/cookie-regulation-cnil/">CNIL</a> (France&#8217;s data protection authority) is <a href="https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience">introducing a new self-assessment framework</a> for analytics tools seeking to rely on consent exemption.</p>



<p>Previously, the CNIL provided a list of pre-approved analytics solutions. Under the updated approach, <strong>all analytics providers must now evaluate their own compliance</strong> against standardised criteria outlined in the CNIL&#8217;s revised guide: <em>&#8220;<a href="https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf">Cookies : solutions pour les outils de mesure d&#8217;audience&#8221;</a>. </em>Self-assessment is not a CNIL certification and does not prevent the CNIL from reaching a different conclusion in an investigation or audit.</p>



<p>Instead of informal interpretations, analytics providers will be expected to demonstrate compliance against clearly defined criteria.</p>



<p>What this means in practice:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Compliance will be evaluated against explicit, published criteria</li>



<li>Responsibility shifts more clearly to the analytics controller and the analytics solution provider</li>



<li>Documentation, transparency, and configuration clarity become critical</li>
</ul>



<p></p>

<p>Matomo has long been recognised by the CNIL as a privacy-compliant analytics solution. Under the new framework, we&#8217;re preparing detailed self-assessment documentation for early 2026 to help you demonstrate compliance.</p>



<p>The goal is simple: make compliance verifiable, auditable, and understandable, not interpretive.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">EU: the Digital Omnibus initiative could reshape analytics rules</h2>



<p>At the European Union (EU) level, the European Commission adopted the <a href="https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/simpler-digital-rules-help-eu-businesses-grow-2025-11-19_en">Digital Omnibus initiative</a> last month.</p>



<p>If passed into law, it would bring substantial amendments to the <a href="https://matomo.org/gdpr-analytics/">GDPR</a>, the ePrivacy Directive, and other <a href="https://matomo.org/blog/2025/03/data-privacy-regulations-essential-knowledge-for-global-business/">data privacy regulations</a> across Europe, potentially taking effect in 2026.</p>



<div class="wp-block-spacer" style="height: 20px;"></div>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Proposed changes worth watching</strong></h3>



<p>Some amendments include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>A narrower definition of </strong><a href="https://matomo.org/blog/2024/05/gdpr-sensitive-personal-data/"><strong>&#8220;personal data&#8221; </strong>in the GDPR</a></li>



<li><strong>Allowing certain uses of personal data, including AI training</strong>, without explicit consent</li>
</ul>



<p>But one proposed amendment stands out and is particularly relevant for analytics teams.</p>



<p>It would <strong>exempt consent for accessing or storing data on terminal equipment when it is strictly necessary</strong> for creating aggregated audience measurement, provided that:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>The website controller carries out the analytics for itself: you, as the site owner, collect the data to understand your audience, not a third party.</li>



<li>The data is used solely for your own purposes.</li>



<li>The data isn&#8217;t combined with other datasets.</li>



<li>The analytics provider does not reuse the data for its own purposes: your analytics tool doesn&#8217;t siphon off your data for its own commercial interests.</li>
</ul>



<p>This distinction is critical. <strong>It would explicitly favour first-party, privacy-focused analytics models like Matomo Analytics</strong>, and exclude solutions like Google Analytics that monetise, enrich, or repurpose analytics data across multiple clients.</p>



<div class="wp-block-spacer" style="height: 40px;"></div>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Where the analytics line is drawn</strong></h2>



<p>The exemption would not apply to solutions where:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Analytics data is combined with other datasets</li>



<li>The analytics provider reuses the data for its own commercial or secondary purposes</li>
</ul>



<p>In other words, platforms that monetise, enrich, or repurpose analytics data across multiple clients, like Google Analytics, would fall outside the scope of this exemption.</p>



<p>This would effectively favour privacy-focused analytics tools and exclude surveillance-based platforms that monetise user data.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">United Kingdom: PECR updates to simplify consent-free analytics</h2>



<p>In the UK, the <a href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/">Data (Use and Access) Act 2025</a> introduces updates to the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR). These changes are expected to <strong>make it easier to use <a href="https://matomo.org/blog/2025/06/privacy-friendly-analytics/">privacy-friendly analytics</a> without requiring consent</strong>, as long as certain safeguards are met.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">When consent-free analytics will be allowed under the updated PECR</h3>



<p>In practical terms, analytics may be used without consent where:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="1">
<li><strong>The use is strictly statistical</strong>, to improve the website or service</li>



<li><strong>Data is not shared or reused</strong> for any other purpose</li>



<li><strong>Users receive clear and comprehensive information</strong> about the tracking</li>



<li><strong>Users have a simple way to opt out</strong>, and have not done so</li>
</ol>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Current status</h3>



<p>These PECR-related changes <strong>aren&#8217;t yet in force</strong>. They&#8217;re expected to apply as part of a later rollout of Part 5 of the Act, likely in early 2026.</p>



<p>The ICO (UK&#8217;s data protection regulator) is also expected to publish updated Direct Marketing and Privacy and Electronic Communications Guidance, which will clarify the limits of this exemption. Initial publication is anticipated <a href="https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-plans-for-commencement">in winter 2025/2026</a>.</p>



<p><strong>What we&#8217;ll do:</strong> Once the ICO guidance is released, we&#8217;ll confirm the best ways to configure Matomo to comply with the new UK consent-exemption criteria, ensuring teams can confidently align with UK-specific requirements.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What this means for your analytics strategy</h2>



<p>Across France, the EU, and the UK, one trend is consistent. These regulatory shifts share a common thread: <strong>privacy-first analytics is becoming the standard, not the exception</strong>.</p>



<p>If you&#8217;re using an analytics tool that:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">

</ul>
